## When this applies

The letter looks official but something is wrong. Common signs:

- The sending address is not the domain the agency uses, for example a free mailbox or a
  misspelled agency name.
- It asks for a password, a code, a card number or a copy of an identity document.
- It asks for payment by gift card, transfer to a personal name, or cryptocurrency.
- It sets a very short deadline and threatens immediate suspension or legal action.
- It asks us to confirm banking details by clicking a link.
- The reference number does not exist in our files, or the letterhead is a poor copy.

## Steps

1. Do not open the attachments and do not click the links.
2. Take a screenshot of the sender and the message headers.
3. Check the sender address against the contact we already hold for that agency. A
   message from a real agency comes from a domain we have seen before.
4. Report the letter to the owner the same day.
5. Report it to the agency through the channel on their official site, if the owner
   agrees.
6. Keep the letter in a fraud folder. Do not delete it.

## If you cannot settle it

Any letter that asks for credentials, cards or a payment to a new account escalates. It
is never filed and never paid.
