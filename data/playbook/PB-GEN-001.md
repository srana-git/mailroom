## When this applies

The letter is a standard notice from an organisation we deal with, and the mail is
expected. Examples: a rate notice, a general information update, a confirmation of a
change we requested, a statement with a zero balance.

## Steps

1. Read the letter.
2. Confirm it matches something we already expect. An unexpected letter from a familiar
   sender is not routine.
3. Check for any date that starts a period running.
4. File it under the sending organisation.
5. If nothing was expected, verify the sender before filing. Unexpected official mail is
   the cover that fraud uses.
